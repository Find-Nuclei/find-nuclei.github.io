Video script — Analytics & Phenotyping ​

Target length: ~2 minutes (this one earns more time than annotations — the phenotype flow is the payoff) Audience: Cell biologists with CellProfiler / QuPath / CP exports sitting on disk Call to action: Try it with your own CSV

Cold open (0:00 – 0:10) ​

Visual:

  • A WSI with segmentation overlay visible, all cells in a single flat color (orange, say).
  • Pause 1 second.
  • Cut: same image, cells now colored by phenotype — Small/Mid/Large in blue/orange/red. Legend card in the corner.
  • Pause 1 second.

Voiceover:

"Segmentation is the hard part. Find Nuclei Viewer colors it by whatever measurement you care about — area, intensity, shape — and groups cells into named phenotypes. All in the browser. All interactive."

Load a CSV (0:10 – 0:30) ​

Visual:

  • Labels tab open in sidebar. Click a label row — it expands.
  • Analytics section visible with "Load CSV" button.
  • Click. OS file picker opens briefly.
  • Pick nuclei-measurements.csv.
  • Analytics section updates: "nuclei-measurements.csv · 329 obj · 12 col".
  • Click the info (ⓘ) button. Modal opens showing columns with min/max/mean statistics.
  • Close modal.

Voiceover:

"Any per-object CSV from CellProfiler, QuPath, FIJI, or napari works. The viewer auto-detects the object-ID column, profiles every column, and shows you the stats. No server, no upload — your data stays on your machine."

Color by column — heatmap (0:30 – 0:50) ​

Visual:

  • Click the color swatch on the label row. Popover opens.
  • Click "Heatmap" tab.
  • Dropdown: select area_px.
  • Ramp dropdown: switch between viridis → magma → coolwarm. Canvas re-colors in real time.
  • A histogram with a dual-range slider appears. Drag the max handle left — the contrast tightens on mid-range cells, outliers collapse to the top color.
  • Close popover.
  • Canvas shows cells in a smooth viridis gradient. Legend in bottom-right shows the ramp with min/max labels.

Voiceover:

"Heatmap mode — pick a numeric column, pick a ramp. Viridis is the default because it's perceptually uniform, but magma, inferno, coolwarm, and turbo are all here. If outliers compress your signal, override the range with the slider."

Filters (0:50 – 1:10) ​

Visual:

  • Scroll to the Filters section. Click "+ Add filter".
  • Popover lists columns. Pick eccentricity.
  • Filter row appears with a histogram. Drag the left handle in — elongated cells disappear from the canvas.
  • Click "+ Add filter" again. Pick a string column, say is_mitotic.
  • Two checkboxes: "true" and "false". Uncheck "false" — only mitotic cells visible.

Voiceover:

"Filters hide cells that don't match. Numeric columns get a range slider. String columns get checkboxes. Multiple filters compose with AND — every filter must pass."

Phenotypes (1:10 – 1:45) ​

Visual:

  • Remove the two filters (click × on each).
  • Scroll to Phenotypes. Click "+ Add phenotype".
  • A new group "Phenotype 1" appears. Click the name — rename to "Small".
  • Click the swatch — pick blue from the preset grid.
  • Click "+ Add filter" inside the group. Pick area_px. Drag range slider to low end.
  • Canvas immediately colors small cells blue.
  • Add two more phenotypes: "Mid" (orange), "Large" (red). Each with an area_px range.
  • Scroll up. Canvas now shows three-color phenotype overlay.
  • Legend bottom-right updates with Small / Mid / Large rows.

Voiceover:

"Phenotypes turn filters into named populations. Give the group a name, a color, and conditions — filter by area, by intensity, by a category column, or any combination. First match wins, so reorder by dragging to get the logic right."

Show unmatched + palette toggle (1:45 – 1:55) ​

Visual:

  • Toggle "Show unmatched objects (gray)" off — a handful of grayed cells disappear.
  • Toggle it back on.
  • Colors toggle: "Assigned → Palette". The three groups swap to Tableau colors.
  • Flip back to "Assigned".

Voiceover:

"Unmatched cells show in gray for context, or hide entirely. Colors come from your own palette, or from a built-in categorical one."

Persistence and sharing (1:55 – 2:10) ​

Visual:

  • Click somewhere on the toolbar — the share link icon. A share modal or copied-to-clipboard toast.
  • Reload the page (show the reload).
  • CSV still loaded, phenotypes still active, view restored.

Voiceover:

"Everything persists in your browser. Reload, come back tomorrow, pick up where you left off. Share a link and a reviewer opens the same image with the same colors — annotations and view state come along in the URL."

Close (2:10 – 2:20) ​

Visual: Final shot of the image with the phenotype overlay, legend visible, fullscreen.

Voiceover:

"Open it at find-nuclei dot github dot io. Your CSV, your image, your classification — in thirty seconds."

On-screen text: find-nuclei.github.io

Production notes ​

  • CSV choice: use a CellProfiler Nuclei.csv or similar — real-world, with recognizable column names like AreaShape_Area, Intensity_MeanIntensity_DAPI.
  • Pre-load the image so the cold open doesn't spend time on loading screens. You can edit a clean re-take of the image into the intro.
  • Ramp tour: when cycling ramps, space them by ~1 second each so viewers can see the visual difference.
  • Don't over-explain: the voiceover assumes the viewer understands what segmentation is. If we need a 101 explainer, that's a separate video.
  • Phenotype demo data: pre-figure out three groups that segment cleanly — Small < 100 px², Mid 100-500, Large > 500 — so the phenotype demo has obvious visual separation.

Example: a Plate with Analysis Results ​

Most examples show pixels. This one shows what an analysis adds on top: masks for every nucleus and cell, and a measurement table behind each of them. It opens in one click, straight from our site.

Open the example in the viewer

Or open the Example Gallery and pick BBBC013 FKHR translocation. The Analytical Results filter shows only examples that come with results.

What is in it ​

The plate. BBBC013 from the Broad Bioimage Benchmark Collection. Human U2OS cells express FKHR-GFP, a protein that moves from the cytoplasm into the nucleus when the PI3K pathway is blocked. Two inhibitors run as dose series across the plate: Wortmannin in rows A to D, LY294002 in rows E to H. Two channels: DRAQ for DNA, and FKHR-GFP.

The results. One analysis run, three compartments:

MaskObjectsMade with
nuclei18,648StarDist (2D_versatile_fluo)
cells15,331Cellpose (cyto3)
cytoplasm15,272each cell minus its nucleus

Each mask carries its own table: area, shape and intensity for every object, plus well and plate summaries.

Look around ​

  1. The plate opens inverted on white, zoomed in on one well. Cells are coloured by the phenotype the analysis gave them: positive, negative or excluded. Nuclei are drawn in grey on top.
  2. The Labels tab lists the three masks under one heading, the analysis run. Press the on a mask to show or hide it.
  3. Click the nuclei row to open its details. The Analytics section lists the measured columns.
  4. Colour the nuclei by a column, filter them by value, or build a phenotype. See Analytics & Phenotyping.
  5. Zoom out to see all 96 wells, or pick a well from the Navigation tab. See OME-Zarr Plates.

A good first column: colour the nuclei by ch1_mean, the mean FKHR-GFP inside each nucleus. In wells with more inhibitor, more of the signal sits in the nucleus.

Who made the masks ​

We ran the Find Nuclei Analysis Tool over the plate. The masks and measurements are our output, not part of the original collection.

Use your own results ​

Everything here is plain files on a web server: an OME-Zarr plate, and next to it a folder with masks, Parquet tables and a manifest. Put yours on any host that answers the browser directly, and the same link pattern opens them:

?url=<plate>&analyticsUrl=<run-folder>/

The Analytics Data Format describes the files, from a single CSV up to a full run. Sharing Views covers what else a link can carry.

For a walkthrough of the analysis itself on this same screen, read HCS Analytics in the Browser: 18,648 Nuclei, Zero Uploads.

3D Rendering ​

A z-stack is a volume. The 2D view shows it one plane at a time, the way a microscope does, or flattened by a projection. 3D shows the whole thing at once: three orthogonal planes with a shared crosshair, next to a volume rendering you can turn with the mouse.

3D is part of the same viewer. There is nothing to install and nothing to upload. It opens from the image you already have open, and it takes your channels, your place and your annotations with it.

What you need

3D needs a desktop browser with WebGPU, and an image that has more than one z-section. It opens the images the viewer opens: from a URL, a local folder, an .ozx or .zip file (from your disk or its address), or a token-protected store. See Requirements and limits for the full list. Everything else in the viewer works without it.

Try it now. Two public datasets with their segmentation, each one click away. Nothing to install and nothing to upload.

Click an image to open that view in the viewer, then turn it, zoom in and change the settings. Under each image are the settings, so you can repeat the look on your own data. Both datasets are CC BY 4.0, credited under Examples.

Open an image in 3D ​

2D is the default. An image always opens in 2D, and 3D is one click away.

  • The 3D button. In the Navigation panel, next to the Z slider, the 2D | 3D switch has a 3D button.
  • The 3D badge. The header shows the mode. Click the badge to switch.
  • The offer at open. When an image has z-sections, a notice over the image says "This image has 42 z-sections" and offers Open in 3D. It goes away on its own after about 12 seconds. The cross dismisses it for the rest of the session.
  • A link. A link with mode=3d opens in 3D. See Link parameters.

To go back, click 2D in the switch or the 3D badge.

If 3D cannot open an image, the 3D button shows disabled and its tooltip says why. If the browser has no WebGPU, or the image has one z-section or is RGB, or you are on a phone or tablet, the switch is not offered at all.

What comes with you ​

A switch keeps your place. These carry over, and carry back when you return to 2D:

  • the channels: on or off, colour, contrast window and gamma
  • the z-plane and the time point
  • INV and the background
  • your annotations
  • your labels: shown or hidden, colour, opacity, Fill and Contour

Each mode also keeps its own view. Switch from 2D to 3D and back, and 2D is at the same zoom, pan and plane. Switch from 3D to 2D and back, and 3D has the same camera and panel layout. The side panel keeps its tab and scroll position the same way. This is kept per image for the session. A different image starts fresh.

Recent remembers the mode too. An image you left in 3D reopens in 3D from Recent. One you left in 2D reopens in 2D.

Plates ​

A field of a plate opens in 3D when it has z-sections. The plate navigation (the well map, the arrows and the field list) stays in the Navigation panel. Pick a well or a field there, and that field opens in 3D at the same z and time point.

In 3D a plate has only Field view. Well and Plate are disabled, with "Available in 2D" as their tooltip. If you are in Well or Plate view when you switch, the 2D view first moves to the field you are looking at.

See HCS Plates for the plate views.

The four panels ​

3D draws four panels:

PanelWhat it shows
XYThe plane you know from 2D, at the crosshair's z
XZA side cut through the stack, at the crosshair's y
YZThe other side cut, at the crosshair's x
3DThe volume rendering

The three planes share one crosshair. In Crosshair mode, click or drag on a plane to move it. The other planes and the volume follow. The Position card holds the X, Y and Z sliders and, on a time-lapse image, T. Press M to fold or open it.

Anatomical orientation ​

Some images record which way is up in the body: superior and inferior, left and right, anterior and posterior. This comes from the OME-Zarr metadata (the anatomical orientation proposal, RFC-4). When an image has it, 3D uses it:

  • The ends of each axis carry their anatomical names, at the edges of the planes, in the Position card and on the axis lock buttons of the 3D panel.
  • Medical on the canvas toolbar shows the planes the radiological way: superior up, and the patient's right on the left of the screen.
  • The image's Info dialog lists the orientation.

Without that metadata, the axes are named x, y and z, and Medical is disabled.

Layouts ​

The layout buttons on the canvas toolbar choose how the panels share the space:

  • 2×2 shows four equal panels. This is the default.
  • 1+3 shows one big panel and three small ones.
  • 1 shows one panel.

The buttons labelled XY, XZ, YZ and 3D pick the big panel. In the 2×2 layout, pressing one of them switches to 1+3 with that panel large. Opened without a main parameter, the big panel is the 3D one.

Moving around ​

WhereAction
A plane, Crosshair mode (default)Click or drag to move the crosshair. The wheel moves the slice. Cmd or Ctrl + wheel zooms. Shift + drag pans
A plane, Pan modeDrag pans. The wheel zooms. Cmd or Ctrl + click moves the crosshair
The 3D panelDrag to orbit. Shift + drag, or right drag, pans. The wheel zooms. Double-click resets

Other controls on the canvas toolbar:

  • Sync zoom makes the planes zoom together. Sync position makes them pan together, and on, they centre on the crosshair.
  • Medical uses the medical plane convention, with the superior side up. It needs anatomical orientation in the image's metadata, and is disabled without it.
  • Reset fits the planes and resets the 3D camera.

On the 3D panel, Free, X, Y and Z lock the orbit to one axis. Front, Side and Top jump to a quick view. Outlines frames the three crosshair planes.

The full list of keys is in the viewer's Help dialog, under ? or F1. A few are worth knowing now: 1 to 9 turn channels on and off, + and - zoom the plane under the mouse, 0 fits it, and , and . step through time.

MIP and Composite ​

The render mode sits on the 3D panel, next to the scene buttons.

MIP (maximum intensity projection) shows the brightest value along each ray through the volume. It is the 3D version of the maximum projection in Z-Projection. Use it for bright, sparse signal: nuclei, neurons, axons, puncta. It follows each channel's contrast window. Labels are not part of this brightest-value picture: they look as they do in Composite, drawn over it. See Labels in 3D.

Composite accumulates colour and opacity along each ray, front to back. Near structures hide far ones, so a surface looks like a surface. Use it for solid or layered objects, such as a skull or a carp in CT. Which values count as solid is set by the opacity ramp.

The same volume, both ways. These are the nuclei of a small cell cluster, 236 planes, two channels.

When the image opens, one channel starts in Composite and several channels start in MIP. Change it with one click. The link remembers the mode when it differs from that default (rm=mip or rm=composite).

The scene buttons next to it choose what the 3D panel draws:

  • Volume: the volume only
  • Slices: the three crosshair planes only
  • Both: the volume in front of the planes
  • None: no image, only annotations, the crosshair and outlines

In the Settings card of the Channels tab, Brightness has a slider. In Composite the card also shows LIGHT, which turns on gradient shading, and a Density slider. MIP does not use them, so it hides them; their values are kept and come back when you switch to Composite. Brightness also acts on labels in the volume. LIGHT and Density act on the channels only: labels have their own LIGHT in the Labels tab, and their Opacity alone sets how solid they look.

For a CT image, the card starts with a row of presets: Brain, Soft tissue, Lung and Bone. One click sets the contrast window, the opacity ramp, density, brightness and render mode together.

Dragging is kept fast on purpose. While the view moves, the volume is drawn coarser, with fewer pixels and fewer samples. A moment after you stop, it redraws at full quality. The High Quality button in the 3D toolbar, next to None, keeps full quality while you move. It asks more of the graphics card, so on a large volume dragging can feel slower. The choice is remembered in your browser.

Channels in 3D ​

In View Options, the Channels tab works as in 2D: toggle a channel, change its colour, set its contrast window. The shown channels blend into one volume.

3D draws up to four channels at once. An image can have more. List them all, show any four. If you turn on a fifth, 3D refuses and says "3D shows up to 4 channels at once. Hide one first." If 2D was showing more than four when you switched, 3D keeps the first four and shows the same notice.

Heavy reads ​

3D reads the channels you show from the server. For a large volume with several channels, that is a lot of data. When 3D would read two or more channels and more than about 250 MB, a hint offers to keep one:

3D will read 2 channels (about 380 MB). Show only EGFP?

Click Yes and 3D hides the other channels, so it reads one. Click No and it reads them all. The hint shows once per image in a session and never blocks the view. When you go back to 2D, the channels 3D hid are hidden there too.

INV and BW ​

INV in the Settings card flips dark and bright, as in 2D, and it carries over with the switch in both directions. BW in the same card shows the composite in greyscale.

Memory for 3D ​

The 3D memory slider in the Settings card sets how much of your graphics card 3D may use for one image, in steps of 0.5 GB. The default is 1 GB. Your browser remembers the setting. More memory lets 3D read the image at a finer level, with more voxels. Less memory is lighter on the card.

Browsers do not tell a page how much graphics memory there is, so the slider's top comes from what they do report. Where the browser gives the computer's memory (Chrome and Edge, up to 8 GB), the top follows it. Otherwise it is 4 GB. It is never more than the card's largest 3D texture can hold.

A very large image can look coarse at any setting. The card also limits the size of one 3D texture, usually 2048 voxels per side. When that limit, not memory, decides the detail, 3D says so over the view, with the limit and how coarse the image is, for example 1/32 detail. The 3D memory slider then notes that more memory will not add detail. Full detail needs a smaller image.

If the card runs out of memory anyway, 3D steps the setting down by one notch, opens the image again, and tells you. The lower setting is kept. If it runs out at the smallest setting, 3D says so and suggests fewer channels or labels.

Labels count against the same memory, four bytes for every voxel. When the image and its labels do not fit together, both are read at the next coarser level, so a label always lines up with its image.

The opacity ramp ​

In Composite, every channel card has a 3D opacity ramp. It is separate from the contrast window in 2D. The window says how bright a value looks. The ramp says how opaque it is.

Values at or below the ramp's low end add no opacity. Values at or above its high end are fully opaque. In between, opacity rises in a straight line. Move the low end up to cut noise and haze. Move the high end down to make a structure more solid.

Two buttons sit on the section's header:

  • 2D starts the ramp at the channel's 2D contrast window. This is the quickest way to a first result.
  • FIT zooms the slider to the ramp's range. When the ramp is narrow compared with the full intensity range, a slider covering everything is too coarse to adjust. FIT makes it fine. Press it again to see the full range. It fits itself the first time a narrow ramp appears.

In MIP the ramp section is not shown.

Annotations across the switch ​

Annotations are one set for 2D and 3D. Switching keeps them.

  • They are kept per image. On a plate, they are kept per field.
  • Both modes read and write the same saved set. A switch saves what the mode you leave holds, and the other mode loads it.

In 3D you can draw on any plane. The second bar holds the tools: Select (V), Polygon (P, drag for freehand), Wand (W), Brush (R) and Distance (D). Under More are Point (B), Line (L), Angle (G) and Text (T). Cmd or Ctrl + click still moves the crosshair. Escape cancels and Cmd + Z undoes.

A shape is full on its own slice and faint on the other slices of its plane. It also shows where it crosses the two other planes. Lines and outlines show in 3D too.

Structure mode outlines one structure over several slices, and its volume shows in 3D. Switch between Annotation and Structure on the second bar.

See Annotations for the tools, templates and export.

Labels in 3D ​

A label image is a segmentation: one integer for every voxel, one number for every object. See Labels. 3D shows every label that 2D lists for the image: the ones embedded in it, the ones you add with Add Local or Add URL, and the ones of a plate field. On a plate, press Discover in 2D first, as there. 3D reads a label the same way it reads its image.

In 3D, the Labels tab of the side panel is the same Labels panel as in 2D. Add Local, Add URL, Refresh, Filter, Search and Group By work as they do in 2D. So do the eye, the name and the colour swatch. There is one set of labels for both modes: a change in 3D is a change in 2D, and the other way round.

The swatch holds:

ControlIn 3D
Solid, RandomThe colours 2D shows. Heatmap and Category need a measurement table, and are set in 2D. 3D shows the same colours
OpacityHow much the label covers the image. In the volume it alone sets how solid the label looks; the Channels tab's Density does not change it
Render: FILL, CONTOURIn the XY, XZ and YZ planes, Fill paints each object and Contour draws its outline, as in 2D. In the volume, Fill with Contour draws a black line where objects touch, so you can tell them apart, and leaves the faces you look at in their colour. Contour alone draws each object's shell. With both off, the label is hidden
WidthNext to CONTOUR: how thick the contour is, from 1 to 5 voxels, growing inward from each object's edge. 1 is the thinnest. A wider contour on a large image takes a few seconds to appear; the thin one shows meanwhile. It applies to the planes and to Volumetric labels, not to Surface: surfaces are drawn whole, and the swatch says so
Look: VOLUMETRIC, SURFACEHow the 3D panel draws the label. See below. Only 3D uses it

Above the list, Label brightness scales the colour of every label in 3D, from 0.25 to 4. At 1 the colours are the ones 2D shows. It belongs to this 3D session and does not change 2D. Next to it, SMOOTH draws label edges smooth instead of as voxels, and LIGHT shades Volumetric labels by their own surface, in MIP and in Composite. This LIGHT is the labels' own: the LIGHT in the Channels tab changes only the channels. Surface labels are always shaded. Your browser remembers both switches. A link made in 3D carries them when you turned them off, so the person who opens it sees the labels as you did (Link parameters).

Surface is the default: a label opens in 3D with each object drawn as a closed surface. 3D builds the surfaces in the background and shows them as they are ready, the largest objects first, so the view stays usable while they build. Until they are all built, the corner of the 3D panel says how far it is, for example building surfaces… 120 of 163, and the line goes when they are done. If 3D cannot build them, the label says so in the Labels tab; hide it and show it again to try again. They are the same kind of closed surface that Structures build. A label with many thousands of objects takes longer to build. Volumetric colours the volume instead, each object in its own colour, blended with the image channels. It does no work per object, so it suits a label with a great many objects. Your choice is kept with the label and travels in a link.

A label you never set opens as Surface, and so does one saved before Surface became the default. A link made in 3D says which labels are Surface, so it opens as it was sent, Volumetric labels included, however old it is. The same holds for the masks of an analysis run, in a link and when you reopen the image from Recent.

The same cells, a zebrafish lateral line primordium, seen from the same camera. The image channels are off, so only the label shows: its Solid white at full opacity, with the labels' LIGHT on. The first row is Volumetric, the second is Surface.

Volumetric

Surface

Volumetric: use Fill to see where the label lies, Contour to see inside each object, and Fill + Contour to tell touching objects apart. Surface: lower the opacity to see through the cells, or use Random colours to tell each cell from the next.

Labels look the same in MIP and in Composite. In MIP they are laid over the channels' picture, so an opaque label hides the channels behind it: lower its opacity to see them through it.

The planes show the label as 2D does, as fill or as outline, in the same colours. On a time series the label follows the time point.

Measurement filters and phenotypes you set in 2D apply: 3D never shows an object that 2D hides. Set them in the Labels tab in 2D. Hiding a label in the Labels tab takes it out of the volume and the planes.

Limits:

  • Four Volumetric labels at once. Turning on a fifth Volumetric label is refused with a notice. Surface labels do not count toward the four. The XY, XZ and YZ panels show the first four shown labels, whatever their look. If 2D showed more than four Volumetric labels when you switched, 3D starts with four, and the rest come back as shown when you return to 2D.
  • Object IDs up to 4,294,967,295. The graphics card colours 32-bit IDs. Labels stored as 8 to 64-bit integers are read, signed or unsigned, and 64-bit ones are narrowed to 32 bits. If a label has an ID above what the card can colour, the Labels tab says so for that label instead of drawing a wrong colour. 2D still shows it.
  • Lined up with the image. 3D reads a label at the level that matches the image's. A label with fewer pyramid levels than its image, or a base level half the size, still lines up. A label with a single z-section, a 2D segmentation of a z-stack, shows on every plane.
  • Memory. Labels count against 3D memory.

Link parameters ​

A link with mode=3d opens in 3D. Without mode, it opens in 2D. The 3D view also reads these:

ParameterMeaning
layoutgrid (default), main or single
mainThe big panel: xy, xz, yz or v3d (default v3d)
rmRender mode: mip or composite. Left out, the image's default
camThe 3D camera. Either cam=<yaw>,<pitch>[,<zoom>], a turn from the start view in degrees, or ten numbers, the whole camera as Link writes it
slWhat the 3D panel draws: volume (default), slices, both or none
lit0 turns the Channels tab's LIGHT off. On is the default. The labels' LIGHT is llit
den, briDensity and Brightness, from 0.1 to 10. Both are 1 by default
bw1 turns BW on
lsm0 turns off SMOOTH for the labels. On is the default. In the link, it wins over what your browser remembers for that view and does not change what your browser remembers
llit0 turns off the labels' LIGHT (the Labels tab's, not the Channels tab's). On is the default. It wins over your browser's choice the same way
rampThe 3D opacity ramps that differ from a channel's contrast window, as ramp=<channel index>:<low>:<high>, in data units. Separate several with commas: ramp=0:37:181,1:7:80
labelsLabels that are not part of the image, by their web address: a label image, a labels/ folder, or an analysis bundle or run, anything Add URL takes. Separate several with commas. They load when the link opens, so the person who opens it sees them. Only web addresses travel (an s3:// address goes as its https form); labels you added from your disk stay on your machine. If you already have the same labels, they take the look the link shows. You can write one by hand: ?url=<image address>&labels=<label address>&mode=3d opens the image in 3D with those labels shown. In an embed, labels means something else: which labels to show
l3sThe labels shown as Surface, named as in l; the labels it does not list are Volumetric. A link made in 3D always has it, empty (l3s=) when every label is Volumetric. A link without it leaves each label as it is, which is Surface unless you chose Volumetric
lsHow each shown label is drawn, as ls=<label>:<opacity>:<render>[:<colour>]. The label is named as in l, the image's own labels included. Opacity is 0 to 1. Render is f (Fill), c (Contour), fc or n (neither). A contour wider than 1 has its width after the c: fc3, c5 (1 to 5; no digit is 1). A colour in hex means Solid. Without one, the label is Random
lvColours a label by a measurement, as lv=<label>:<mode>:<column>[:<ramp>], with mode category or heatmap. It comes with analyticsUrl, which holds the measurements
sync, syncpos0 turns off Sync zoom or Sync position
orientmedical for the medical plane convention
pm, ptPointer mode (pan) and the planes' transforms
tThe time point

Link in 3D carries your view: the camera (turn, zoom and target), MIP or Composite, what the 3D panel draws, the layout, the labels you added from a web address, the labels you show as Surface, how each shown label is drawn (opacity, Solid or Random colour, Fill and Contour), and the render settings: the Channels tab's LIGHT, Density, Brightness, BW, the labels' SMOOTH and LIGHT, and each channel's 3D opacity ramp. Open the link and you see what you saw. A setting still at its default adds nothing, so a link you did not change stays short. That includes the labels' SMOOTH and LIGHT: a link that leaves them out opens with your browser's own choice for them. High Quality and 3D memory are about your machine and are not in the link. A link made before this still opens as it did.

A link that cannot open in 3D opens in 2D, with a notice that says why. That is the case on a phone or tablet, in a browser without WebGPU, and for an image with one z-section or RGB.

See Sharing Views for how links work in general. In 3D the header's Link button copies the 3D link, and Screenshot saves the 3D view.

Requirements and limits ​

  • A browser with WebGPU. Without it, 3D is not offered, and its code is never downloaded.
  • Desktop only. On a phone or tablet, the viewer stays in 2D.
  • A z-stack. The image needs more than one z-section and must not be RGB.
  • Where the image comes from. 3D opens an image from a web address, from a local folder (Chrome and Edge, the same browsers that open folders in 2D), from an .ozx or .zip file on your disk or at an address (a Dropbox file link included), or from a token-protected store. If 3D loses access to a folder (the browser let its permission lapse) or to a token-protected store (the token expired), a notice over the view says so and offers Allow access or Sign in. Once you have, 3D reopens where you were. If an image cannot open in 3D at all, a message says so and the viewer stays in 2D.
  • Four channels at once. See Channels in 3D.
  • A connection. 3D is loaded the first time you switch, and the offline install does not include it. 2D keeps working offline. If the 3D code cannot be loaded, for example right after a new release, the viewer asks you to reload and stays in 2D.
  • Heavy reads. Large volumes take time and bandwidth. See Heavy reads.

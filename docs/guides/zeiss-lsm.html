On this page

Zeiss LSM Viewer ​

Open .lsm files from Zeiss LSM 510, 710, 780 and 880 confocals, saved by ZEN or AIM. You get every channel, Z-stack and time series. LSM files shared on Zenodo open by their link.

Open Example LSMOpen Your Own Data

The example is a four-channel confocal LSM. Free. Nothing to install. Your file stays on your computer.

How to Open an LSM File ​

Working with many files?

Several files chosen together, or a zip of them, open as a gallery in every browser. A whole folder opens in Chrome and Edge on a computer, not in Firefox and Safari.

  • One file on your disk, in any browser. Drop it on the viewer, or click Open Image and Choose Files. See Local Files.
  • A folder of LSM files, in Chrome and Edge. One file opens. Several show a list with a picture of each, and files one folder down are listed too. After you pick one, a gallery of pictures under the image takes you to the next. See Folders, Zips and Series.
  • An address: https://, s3:// or gs://, or a Zenodo file link that ends in /content. Paste it under Open Image. See Remote URLs.
  • Private data, with a private link or a token. See Private Images and Slides.

What You Will See ​

  • Channels with the names and colours ZEN gave them, such as ChS1-T1.
  • Spectral (lambda) scans as one channel per wavelength band, each in the colour of its wavelength.
  • Z and time. Sliders step through the planes. A Z-stack can also be viewed in 3D. See 3D Rendering.
  • 8 and 16-bit data, uncompressed, as LSM confocals save it.
  • Pixel size, Z step and time interval from the file. To set your own, see Pixel Size and Magnification.

An LSM re-saved by Fiji or another tool has lost the Zeiss metadata. It opens as a TIFF, with the channels, sizes and pixel size the file itself states. See TIFF and ImageJ Files.

An LSM stores one resolution. The viewer makes the lower resolutions itself, so a large image zooms out and opens in 3D.

If a File Does Not Open ​

The viewer tells you why.

What the file hasWhat the viewer says
A line, point or x-z scanThis LSM is a line, point or x-z scan, which the viewer does not show yet.
Tiles or positions that were never stitchedThis LSM holds unstitched tiles or positions. Open a stitched copy, or convert it to OME-Zarr.
One plane of all channels over 256 MBtoo large to open without a pyramid. Convert it to OME-Zarr to open it here.
A file of 4 GB or moreLSM files of 4 GB or more cannot be read yet. Convert it to OME-Zarr to open it here.
A compressed file, such as LZWThis LSM uses a compression the viewer cannot read yet.
Another pixel typeThis LSM has a pixel type the viewer cannot read yet.

Convert to OME-Zarr to open a very large image with every resolution.

Public LSM Files to Try ​

  • 10-01.lsm: four channels, 16-bit. Salama Lab, Fred Hutchinson Cancer Center, Zenodo 14510432, CC BY 4.0.
  • A spectral Z-stack of a zebrafish eye: 25 wavelength bands, 44 planes. Bruno Schuty and Paola Lepanto, Zenodo 13286257, CC BY 4.0.
  • A time-lapse of growth cones: 60 time points of 30 planes. Özel, Langen, Hassan and Hiesinger, Zenodo 33141, CC0.

For Your IT Team ​

To open a file by address, the server must answer byte-range requests and allow them through CORS. For a bucket, allow GET and the Range header in its CORS rules. What the Server Needs has the details.

Privacy ​

The file is read in your browser. Opening one counts as one opened image of the kind "Zeiss LSM file". The file name and address are never recorded. See the privacy policy.
